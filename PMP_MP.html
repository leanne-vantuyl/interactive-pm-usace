Section 1Project Overview
1.1 Purpose and Authority

This Project Management Plan (PMP) establishes the management framework for the replacement of Building 2247 at Fort Marcus, to be delivered by the USACE Riverside District under Military Programs. The project is authorized under the FY2025 Military Construction (MILCON), Army appropriation and governed by the Project Delivery Business Process (PDBP) as codified in EM 5-1-11.

This PMP is a living document. It is baselined at the close of the planning phase and updated through the change management process (PROC3010) when scope, schedule, or cost baselines are revised. All PDT members are responsible for knowing and working within the framework it establishes.

1.2 Project Background

Building 2247 is the primary administrative facility for the Fort Marcus garrison headquarters. The existing structure, constructed in 1971, has reached the end of its serviceable life. A Facility Condition Assessment completed in October 2023 rated the building at a Facility Condition Index (FCI) of 0.62, below the Army threshold for renovation. The garrison commander and DPW requested MILCON replacement programming in FY2023; the project was included in the FY2025 MILCON program at $14.2M.

1.3 Project Objectives

The project will deliver a new 28,000 gross square foot administrative office complex meeting current UFC standards, providing consolidated workspace for 185 garrison headquarters personnel currently split across three buildings. Specific objectives:

  • Deliver a fully operational facility by 15 October 2026, enabling garrison HQ consolidation before the start of FY2027
  • Meet all applicable UFC 4-010-01 (DoD Minimum Antiterrorism Standards for Buildings) requirements
  • Achieve LEED Silver certification per DoD policy
  • Complete within the $14,200,000 programmed amount, including SIOH

Section 2Scope Baseline
2.1 Product Scope

The project will construct a new two-story administrative office building of approximately 28,000 gross square feet on the existing Building 2247 footprint following demolition of the existing structure. The facility will include open office space, private offices, conference rooms, a secure communications room, accessible restrooms, a building entrance vestibule meeting UFC 4-010-01 standoff requirements, and full mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems.

Demolition of the existing Building 2247 is within scope. Site work within the fenced project boundary is within scope. Utility connections to the existing distribution systems at the property line are within scope.

2.2 Scope Exclusions

The following items are explicitly outside this project's scope and are the responsibility of the installation or other programs:

  • Furniture, fixtures, and equipment (FF&E) -- funded separately by the installation under O&M
  • IT infrastructure and cabling beyond conduit rough-in -- coordinated with DOIM
  • Upgrades to utility distribution systems beyond the property line connection point
  • Relocation of garrison HQ personnel during construction -- DPW responsibility
  • Demolition of Buildings 2248 and 2249 (addressed in a separate project)

3.2 Schedule Basis of Estimate

Schedule durations are based on historical performance data from three comparable MILCON administrative facility projects completed by the Riverside District (FY2019, FY2021, FY2022), vendor input from the pre-solicitation industry day held 18 November 2024, and expert judgment from the A-E project manager (letter of record, 10 January 2025). Design durations reflect resource loading at 80% availability for each technical discipline.

Critical Path: A-E NTP → 35% Design → 100% Design → Solicitation → Award → Construction NTP → Substantial Completion → Final Acceptance. Any delay on a critical path activity delays the project end date. The critical path has zero total float. Near-critical activities (5 days or fewer) are flagged in the project schedule and reviewed weekly.
3.3 Schedule Update Cadence

The project schedule is updated monthly during design and bi-weekly during construction. The PM reviews schedule status at each PDT meeting. Variance from baseline of more than 10 calendar days on any critical path activity triggers a written variance narrative (bottom line, cause, recovery) within 5 business days. Schedule baseline changes require a formal change action under PROC3010.

4.2 Contingency Management

Construction contingency of $574,000 (5% of construction cost) is held in reserve for unknowns that materialize during construction. Use of contingency requires a documented change action identifying the specific condition triggering the draw. Contingency is not project margin and is not available for scope additions. The PM will report contingency balance at each monthly program review.

At 35% design, a revised IGE will be produced. If the IGE exceeds the programmed amount by more than 5%, the PM will present a written gap analysis to the DPM within 10 business days using the Data-Options-Recommendation framework, identifying the three authorized responses: scope reduction, funding increase request, or contingency reduction with documented risk acceptance.

4.3 Earned Value Reporting

EVM is applied during the construction phase per PROC3000. The PM reports Schedule Performance Index (SPI), Cost Performance Index (CPI), Schedule Variance (SV), and Cost Variance (CV) at each monthly program review. A CPI below 0.90 or SPI below 0.85 triggers a written variance narrative within 5 business days. EV data is sourced from the contractor's monthly schedule update and certified payment requests.

Section 5Risk Management
5.1 Risk Management Approach

Risk management for this project follows REF8007G. The Risk Register is maintained in the project SharePoint folder and reviewed at each PDT meeting during design and bi-weekly during construction. Each entry requires all eight REF8007G fields: description, category, probability, impact, owner, trigger, response strategy, and response action. The PM is responsible for ensuring entries are current and triggers are monitored.

The PM will confirm all material decisions in writing within 24 hours: "Per our conversation on [date], we are proceeding with [action]. Please confirm." 6.2 PDT Meeting Norms

PDT meeting agendas are distributed 24 hours in advance. Agendas follow the PROC3000 priority sequence: decision items first, time-sensitive items second, informational items last. Notes including decisions made, action items with owners and due dates, and parking lot disposition are distributed within 24 hours of each meeting.
