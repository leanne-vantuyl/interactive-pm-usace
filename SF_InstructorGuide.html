Your Role in PROSPECT 355

What You Do
  • Open every day with a Kahoot that connects CliftonStrengths to the day's PM content
  • Close every case file with a 10-minute Strengths Debrief + 5-minute Bridge Forward
  • Lead Fusion Lessons (L6, L9, L12) solo -- no CW or MP involvement
  • Close each day with a Day Synthesis that integrates strengths into the day's PM learning
  • Facilitate Action Planning (Day 4) through the strengths lens
Your Pedagogical Intent

Every Strengths Debrief has two jobs: (1) help participants see how their strengths showed up in the case file application, and (2) help them see how the team's collective strengths create a more complete PM capability than any individual brings alone.

The Bridge Forward is not a summary -- it's a forward link. You're not closing the lesson, you're opening the next one through a strengths mindset.

Fusion Lessons (L6 / CF06, L9 / CF09, L12 / CF12) These are SF solo segments. CW and MP do not hand off to you -- you pick up the room directly after a break. No bridging from the previous instructor is needed; you set your own context.

Facilitator Tips

Reading the Room
  • If energy is low after a CW/MP lesson, start with a show-of-hands question before the debrief
  • Name the dominant strength themes you're seeing in the room (Strategic, Achiever, Relator are common in USACE PMs) -- this validates participants before they speak
  • Listen for "I did this because..." -- that's usually a strengths reveal; reflect it back
When the Room Is Resistant
  • Some engineers distrust strengths frameworks; normalize it: "You don't have to believe the label -- just notice what it's pointing at"
  • Use PM language: "Your Analytical theme is what makes you a better risk manager -- that's not soft, that's operational"
  • Pair resistant participants with enthusiasts; the conversation usually shifts them
Timing Discipline
  • Debrief = 10 minutes hard stop; Bridge Forward = 5 minutes
  • If the debrief is running long, it means participants are engaged -- that's good, but keep the Bridge Forward; it's what they'll remember
  • Fusion Lessons have more time flexibility; use it to go deeper, not wider
Action Planning Connection
  • Plant seeds throughout the week: "Notice which strengths you're using most in this case file -- you'll use that on Day 4"
  • By Day 4, participants should be able to name 1-2 strengths they're leaning on most as PMs
  • Action plans that name specific strengths (not just goals) are more likely to be followed through
Avoid Don't position strengths as explaining why someone struggled with a task. Strengths are additive frames, not deficit explanations. "Your Responsibility theme means you hold yourself to a high standard" -- not "you over-committed because of Responsibility."

Strengths Debrief (10 min)
Relator Naturally draws the right people into the PDT; builds trust that makes charter negotiations productive
Command Establishes PM authority early; comfortable holding scope boundaries under pressure
Responsibility Takes charter commitments seriously; this is an asset but can lead to over-commitment in scope
Team Theme The best PDTs combine Relator-type trust-builders with Command-type authority anchors -- neither works as well alone
Individual Prompt
Which of your strengths most influenced how you built out the PDT? Would a different strength have led to a different team?
Team Prompt

Bridge Forward (5 min)

Charter establishes the team and the authority. Tomorrow, you'll decompose the work. Analytical and Strategic themes will show up differently in a WBS than they did in a charter -- the same strength, different application.

Tonight's read-ahead: notice which parts of scope management feel natural to you -- that's usually a strengths signal worth tracking.

Risk Management
Day 3 · After L8 CW Lesson

10
Debrief
5
Bridge Fwd
Strengths Debrief (10 min)
Deliberative The room's best risk identifier; sees what can go wrong before others even ask the question
Strategic Quickly routes to the highest-impact responses; doesn't get lost in low-probability risks
Positivity Keeps the risk exercise constructive; counterbalances Deliberative's tendency toward worst-case thinking
Team Theme The best risk teams see both the threats (Deliberative) and the paths forward (Positivity/Strategic) -- rarely the same person
Individual Prompt

Team Prompt
Did your table have enough "Deliberative energy" to surface the full risk picture? Did you have enough "Positivity energy" to keep moving after identifying them?
Bridge Forward (5 min)

Risk is not just a threat register. Tomorrow you'll flip the frame -- risk as opportunity. Strategic and Futuristic themes are the natural home of opportunity identification. If you have those themes, Day 4 morning is your moment.
