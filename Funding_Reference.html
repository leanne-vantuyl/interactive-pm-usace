Funding and Appropriations for the USACE PM
Color of money, fiscal year rules, appropriation boundaries, and what to do when a scope change crosses a funding line. Covers Civil Works and Military Programs.
The Foundational Rule

Federal appropriations are not just budget lines. They are legal authorities to obligate funds for specific purposes, amounts, and time periods. Spending money outside the purpose, amount, or time period of its appropriation is a violation of the Antideficiency Act -- a federal law with criminal penalties. The PM is not the budget officer, but the PM's decisions create obligations. You need to understand the rules.

Color of Money -- The Three Streams

USACE projects are funded through different appropriation accounts depending on mission area. Each account has its own rules about what it can fund. Mixing them -- even accidentally -- is a legal problem. Know which color your project is working under before you make any spending decision.

MILCON Military Programs
Full name
Military Construction
Used for
New construction, major renovation, and real property acquisition for DoD installations. Single project, single purpose.
Fiscal year
5-year availability. Obligated within 5 years; cancels after.
Key limit
Scope is locked at Congressional authorization. Changes that exceed the approved scope require reprogramming approval.
O&M Military Programs
also RCM / SRM
Full name
Operations and Maintenance; Restoration, Modernization; Sustainment, Restoration and Modernization
Used for
Repair, maintenance, and minor construction below the MILCON threshold. Does not fund new missions or increase square footage.
Fiscal year
1-year availability. Must be obligated in the fiscal year appropriated.
Key limit
Projects at or below $2M may be funded with O&M. Projects above $2M and up to $6M (the unspecified minor construction ceiling under 10 USC 2805) require MILCON with congressional notification. Projects above $6M require a full MILCON appropriation.
CWSF Civil Works
Full name
Civil Works Special Funds (and project-specific appropriations)
Used for
Construction, Operation and Maintenance, Flood Control and Coastal Emergencies, and Investigations -- each is a separate account with separate rules.
Fiscal year
Varies by account. Construction funds are multi-year; O&M is annual. Check the specific appropriation.
Key limit
Civil Works projects are authorized by Congress individually. Work outside the authorized project scope requires a new authorization or a Section 902 modification.
NOTE
The PM does not choose the appropriation. That decision happens at the programming level, long before the PM arrives. What the PM must do is recognize when a decision they are about to make -- a scope change, a delay, a work-around -- might push work into a different appropriation or a different fiscal year. That is the moment to call the budget officer and the KO together.
Fiscal Year Rules -- When Money Dies

Federal funds have a lifespan. A PM who does not understand this will make commitments the government cannot legally honor. The rules differ by appropriation type.

Active
Year 1 (and beyond, for multi-year funds)
Funds can be obligated for new commitments and adjusted. This is the normal operating window. For 1-year O&M funds, this window closes September 30.
Expired
Years 2 through 5 (after obligation period)
Funds cannot be used for new obligations but can still pay legitimate bills on existing contracts. The PM can still process invoices; they cannot authorize new work against expired funds.
Cancelled
Year 6 and beyond
Funds are returned to Treasury and are gone. A bill that arrives after cancellation must be paid from current-year funds -- a violation of the Antideficiency Act if done without authority. This is a serious problem.
CRITICAL
A contractor who finishes work in one fiscal year but does not invoice until the next one can create a cancelled-funds problem. The PM should ensure the contractor submits invoices promptly and that the government processes them before funds expire. "We ran out of time" is not an Antideficiency Act defense.
Appropriation Type Obligation Period Expired Period Cancels After
O&M (Military) MP 1 year (Oct 1 -- Sep 30) 5 years 6 years from appropriation
MILCON MP 5 years 5 years after obligation period 10 years from appropriation
CW Construction CW Varies; often multi-year until project complete 5 years after obligation period closes Check the specific appropriation language
CW O&M CW 1 year (Oct 1 -- Sep 30) 5 years 6 years from appropriation
When a Decision Crosses an Appropriation Boundary

Most of the situations where a PM inadvertently creates a funding violation happen because a scope change, a delay, or a work-around moves work across one of three lines: purpose, amount, or time. The PM does not always see the line until it is crossed.

The Three Limits What It Means PM Decision Point
Purpose Funds appropriated for one purpose cannot be used for another. MILCON funds cannot pay for operational expenses. O&M funds cannot fund new construction above the threshold. When a scope change adds work that looks like a different mission or a different category of work, stop and ask the budget officer whether the appropriation still covers it.
Amount The government cannot obligate more than Congress appropriated for the project. For MILCON, the project is individually authorized at a specific dollar amount. When the project estimate exceeds the authorized amount, the PM cannot simply approve more work. Reprogramming or a new authorization is required.
Time Funds cannot be obligated outside their availability period, and cannot be used to meet obligations incurred before or after that period. When a delay pushes work into a new fiscal year, check whether the funds are still available. A contract modification that carries work into the next FY may need to be funded from a new appropriation.
NOTE
For Civil Works projects, there is a Section 902 limit: the total cost of a project cannot exceed the congressionally authorized amount by more than 20% without new Congressional authorization. The PM should know what the Section 902 ceiling is for their project and flag the KO and the budget officer when estimates approach it.
Reprogramming and Reallocation -- Who Can Move Money

When a project needs more funding than was programmed, or when money needs to move between projects, that is a reprogramming action. The PM does not initiate reprogramming. The PM's role is to identify the need, document it accurately, and escalate it promptly so the people with authority can act.

Action Who Has Authority PM's Role
Minor reprogramming within an account District or Division commander, within delegated limits Identify the need, quantify it, and bring it to the budget officer. Document the reason -- a reprogramming request needs a clear justification.
Reprogramming above the delegated threshold ASA(CW) or ASA(IE&E) depending on mission area Same as above. The PM's documentation feeds the formal request package. The PM should not promise the customer that reprogramming will be approved.
Congressional notification / prior approval Congress, for transfers above statutory thresholds Notify the sponsor. This is a significant delay. The PM should surface this risk early, not after the threshold is crossed.
MILCON scope increase beyond original Congressional authorization MP Requires new or amended Congressional authorization Stop. Brief the district and division. Do not proceed with additional scope until authority is confirmed.
Where Funding Comes From -- The POM Cycle

New PMs often ask "can't we just get more money?" The answer is almost always no, and understanding why requires understanding the budget cycle. By the time a PM is managing a project, the funding for that project was requested and approved 2 to 3 years earlier.

Step What Happens When
Program Objective Memorandum (POM) USACE submits its funding requirements for future years to the DoD budget process. Individual projects are listed and justified. MP About 2 years before the fiscal year being programmed
President's Budget Request DoD submits the consolidated budget request to Congress. Projects included here have survived internal DoD review. Both February of the year before the FY begins
Congressional Authorization and Appropriation Congress authorizes projects (what can be done) and appropriates funds (actual money). For Civil Works, the Energy and Water Development bill. For Military, the NDAA and MILCON Appropriations Act. Both Ideally by October 1; often later
Apportionment and Allotment OMB apportions funds; the Army distributes allotments to commands. The PM's project receives its funding through this chain. Both After appropriation is enacted
Obligation The KO awards a contract or issues a modification. Funds are legally committed. The PM's work scope is now a legal obligation of the government. Both After allotment is received
NOTE
If Congress has not passed an appropriations bill by October 1, the government operates under a Continuing Resolution (CR). Under a CR, agencies typically can only spend at the prior year's rate and cannot start new projects. A PM whose project was counting on new-start funding in October may face a delay of weeks or months. Know whether your project depends on a new appropriation and plan accordingly.
Day-to-Day: What the PM Does and Does Not Do
Situation PM Action
Contractor submits a change proposal that would increase the project cost above the authorized amount Documents the change, estimates the total impact, and immediately flags the budget officer and KO. Does not authorize the change. Informs the sponsor that a reprogramming action may be required before the change can proceed.
Project is running behind schedule and may not obligate all funds before September 30 Raises the risk immediately -- not in the next monthly report, this week. Works with the KO to determine whether funds can be obligated on contract modifications or task orders to preserve them. The budget officer decides whether an obligation strategy is available.
A scope addition is requested that looks like a different mission than the one the project was funded for Does not agree to the addition informally. Routes it to the budget officer and KO with a question: does the current appropriation cover this work? Gets the answer in writing before proceeding.
The sponsor asks the PM to use project funds to buy equipment or furniture for the building Checks the appropriation. MILCON can fund installed equipment; it typically cannot fund loose furniture or operational equipment. O&M rules differ. Route the question to the budget officer before telling the sponsor yes or no.
The PM receives a contractor invoice after the funds have expired Does not simply reject the invoice. Contacts the budget officer immediately. Legitimate invoices for work performed during the obligation period can still be paid from expired funds in the unexpired window. After cancellation, it is a much harder problem.
The PM is told "we have leftover money at the end of the year -- use it" Proceeds carefully. End-of-year spending must still be for a legitimate government need within the purpose of the appropriation. "Use it or lose it" spending on items not related to the project is an Antideficiency Act violation.
Quick Rules
01 Know what color your money is. Before any spending decision, confirm the appropriation: purpose, amount, and time period. If you are not sure, ask the budget officer. This is not optional.
02 The obligation period ends September 30 for 1-year funds. Anything that depends on those funds must be obligated on contract before midnight. Work that starts in October against expired funds is a violation.
03 You cannot move money. You can only document the need and escalate promptly. Reprogramming authority sits above the PM at every level. Surface funding problems early -- the people who can act need time to act.
04 A scope change that costs money is also a funding action. It is not just a contract modification. Before agreeing to anything that changes the cost, check whether the appropriation covers it and whether the authorized amount still holds.
05 "We have money left over" is not a purchasing authority. End-of-year funds must still be obligated for legitimate purposes within the appropriation's scope. Spending money just to avoid returning it is a violation.
06 The POM cycle runs 2 to 3 years ahead of execution. If your project does not have enough funding, the window to fix that through the budget process has likely already passed. Document, escalate, and manage expectations with your sponsor -- not promises you cannot keep.
07 Antideficiency Act violations have criminal penalties. The PM is not the accountable official -- but the PM's decisions create the facts. Document your questions and the answers you received. If something looks wrong, it probably is.
Key Terms
Antideficiency Act (ADA)
Federal law prohibiting agencies from obligating or spending funds in excess of, or in advance of, an appropriation. Violations can result in administrative and criminal penalties.
Obligation
A legally binding commitment of government funds -- typically a contract award or modification. Once obligated, the government must pay if the contractor performs.
Appropriation
A Congressional act that authorizes an agency to incur obligations and make payments for specific purposes, up to a specified amount, during a specified time period.
Color of Money
Informal term for the appropriation type and its associated restrictions. "What color is this money?" means "which appropriation is funding this and what are its rules?"
Reprogramming
Moving funds from one project or program to another within the same appropriation account. Requires authority at some level depending on the amount and type.
Continuing Resolution (CR)
Temporary funding legislation that allows the government to operate when a new appropriations bill has not been passed by October 1. Generally prohibits new starts and limits spending to prior-year rates.
Section 902 Limit CW
Statutory cap on cost growth for Civil Works projects (33 USC 2280). Total cost cannot exceed the congressionally authorized amount by more than 20% without new Congressional authorization. The PM must know the project's Section 902 ceiling and flag the budget officer when estimates approach it.
Minor Construction Threshold MP
Under 10 USC 2805, the ceiling for unspecified minor construction is $6M. Projects at or below $2M may be funded with O&M appropriations. Projects between $2M and $6M use MILCON authority with congressional notification. Projects above $6M require a full MILCON appropriation.
