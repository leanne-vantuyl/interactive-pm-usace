Section 1Project Overview
1.1 Purpose and Authority

This Project Management Plan (PMP) establishes the management framework for the renovation of Building 412, the Riverside District Operations Center, funded under Operations and Maintenance, Civil Works (O&M, CW) appropriations. The project is managed by the USACE Riverside District and governed by the Project Delivery Business Process (PDBP) as codified in EM 5-1-11.

This project is O&M funded, meaning the district is both the program manager and the facility owner. There is no non-federal sponsor and no cost-share obligation. Funding is subject to annual apportionment and cannot be obligated beyond the appropriation period without explicit authority.

O&M vs. MILCON: Unlike MILCON projects, O&M-funded projects do not require Congressional authorization beyond the appropriation itself. However, they are subject to the single-year obligation rule: O&M funds must be obligated in the fiscal year appropriated (with limited exceptions under the bona fide need rule). This shapes the schedule's procurement milestone more tightly than a MILCON project would be.
1.2 Project Background

Building 412 houses the Riverside District's operations center, including the Emergency Operations Center (EOC), the Real Estate field office, and the District Operations section. The building was last renovated in 1998. A facility assessment completed in August 2024 identified structural deficiencies in the mezzanine floor system, inadequate HVAC capacity for the current equipment load in the EOC, and accessibility gaps under the current ADA standard. The District Commander authorized O&M programming in September 2024 for a phased renovation addressing all three deficiencies.

1.3 Project Objectives
  • Complete all structural, HVAC, and accessibility work by 30 September 2026 (end of FY2026) to align with the O&M obligation authority
  • Maintain EOC operational capability throughout construction -- EOC downtime not to exceed 72 consecutive hours at any point
  • Bring all areas into ADA compliance per the current standard (ABA Accessibility Standards, 2017)
  • Complete within the $4,850,000 programmed O&M amount

Section 2Scope Baseline
2.1 Product Scope

The renovation of Building 412 encompasses three work packages, each tied to a deficiency identified in the August 2024 facility assessment:

  • Work Package A -- Structural: Repair and reinforce the mezzanine floor system per the structural engineer's design. Install temporary shoring as required during construction. No change to gross square footage.
  • Work Package B -- HVAC: Replace the EOC HVAC system with a dedicated precision cooling unit sized for current equipment load. Reroute supply and return ductwork. Decommission the legacy unit.
  • Work Package C -- Accessibility: Reconfigure the main entrance vestibule, install a compliant accessible route from the parking area, and upgrade two restrooms to current ABA standard.

All three work packages will be bid as a single contract with separate line items, allowing the district to exercise or defer individual packages if funding is reduced.

2.2 Scope Exclusions
  • Roof replacement (deferred to FY2027 O&M programming)
  • Interior painting beyond construction-disturbed surfaces
  • Furniture replacement or reconfiguration
  • IT/AV infrastructure beyond conduit rough-in associated with Work Package B
  • Work on Building 411 (separate project, separate funding)

O&M Obligation Deadline: All construction work must be substantially complete and accepted by 30 September 2026 to ensure final voucher processing within the O&M appropriation period. The baseline schedule achieves substantial completion by 10 October 2025, providing a 355-day buffer against the FY2026 obligation deadline. The PM will flag any schedule slip exceeding 90 days as a potential obligation risk.
3.2 Schedule Basis of Estimate

Design durations are based on confirmed resource commitments from the structural, mechanical, and architectural section chiefs (letters of record, 15 January 2025). Construction durations are based on RS Means unit cost data adjusted for the local market, vendor input from an industry day held 10 December 2024, and comparison to three similar O&M renovation projects completed by the district in FY2021-2023. Procurement duration reflects the simplified acquisition procedures available for contracts below $7.5M and assumes award within 42 calendar days of solicitation publication.

Higher Contingency Rate: Construction contingency is set at 8% (vs. 5% in the MILCON example) to account for the higher uncertainty associated with renovation work in an occupied building with a 1998-era construction record. Renovation projects consistently carry higher unknown conditions risk than new construction of equivalent scope.
4.2 In-House Design Cost Accounting

Design is performed in-house by the district's structural, mechanical, and architectural sections. Design costs are captured as direct labor charges to the project P2 number. Section chiefs have confirmed resource availability for the 10-week design period. If design scope or duration expands beyond the baseline, the PM will assess whether A-E support is required and present a cost impact analysis to the DPM.

4.3 Contingency Management and Funding Gap Protocol

If the construction IGE (produced at 100% design) exceeds the available construction budget by more than 5%, the PM will present a written gap analysis to the DPM within 10 business days. The three authorized responses are scope reduction (defer one or more work packages to FY2027), funding increase request (supplemental O&M reprogramming), or contingency reduction with documented risk acceptance. The PM will state a recommendation. All three options will be presented regardless of which the PM recommends.
