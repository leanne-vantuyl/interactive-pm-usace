A few minutes here will make the next thirteen case files make more sense — and give you a way to look back on your own thinking once you've finished.

One Project, Thirteen Decisions

Every case file in this course follows the same person, the same project, in order: Maria Chen, a Project Manager at USACE, leading the Building 4521 Renovation from her first uncertain week through closeout. The same colleagues — Dana, Marcus, Pat, Priya, and others — reappear throughout, and decisions made in early case files have consequences that surface later. It is one story, told in thirteen decisions.

How Each Case File Works

Each case file follows the same four-part structure: Training (a few minutes of doctrine, grounded in PDBP/PROC references), Discussion (a short personal reflection — not graded, saved only on your device), Case Study (a branching scenario with three decisions, each with realistic consequences), and a Knowledge Check (5 questions, 60% to pass, with explanations either way).

The Eight Lenses in This Course

A talent is a natural pattern of thinking, feeling, or behaving. Invested in through practice and self-awareness, a talent becomes a strength — something you do well and can apply deliberately. Throughout this course, each case file includes a short reflection written through the lens of YOUR top CliftonStrengths theme — the same scenario, seen through eight different natural approaches, two from each of the four domains below.

Strategic Thinking — how you absorb information and think through possibilities

Analytical — wants to understand the "why" before acting
Strategic — quick to see alternative paths and what they lead to

Relationship Building — how you build and strengthen connections with others

Empathy — senses what others are feeling, often before they say it
Relator — builds trust through close, genuine relationships

Influencing — how you reach a wider audience and move people to act

Command — comfortable taking charge and making a decision
Woo — wins people over, finds connection easily

Executing — how you make things happen and get the job done

Achiever — driven by visible progress and getting things done
Responsibility — feels ownership for commitments, large and small

None of these are better or worse — and you do not need to know your CliftonStrengths results to take this course. If you do, each case file will ask for your top five themes and tailor a short reflection to whichever of the eight above is highest on your list. If you do not, enter N/A and the course works just as well without one.

The Four Domains of CliftonStrengths

CliftonStrengths groups all 34 themes into four broad domains — four different ways people tend to contribute on a team. Most people have strengths spread across more than one domain, and no mix is "better" than another. The full picture, beyond just the eight used in this course:

Strategic Thinking — Analytical, Context, Futuristic, Ideation, Input, Intellection, Learner, Strategic
Relationship Building — Adaptability, Connectedness, Developer, Empathy, Harmony, Includer, Individualization, Positivity, Relator
Influencing — Activator, Command, Communication, Competition, Maximizer, Self-Assurance, Significance, Woo
Executing — Achiever, Arranger, Belief, Consistency, Deliberative, Discipline, Focus, Responsibility, Restorative

Wherever your own top five themes fall, at least one of the eight lenses used in this course is likely to feel familiar. Enter your top five below if you know them, or N/A if you do not — the course works the same either way.

A Strength Overused

Every strength has a shadow side — sometimes called a barrier. An Achiever pushing for closure can miss a detail that needed more time. An Analytical mind seeking certainty can delay a decision past the point it was needed. A few case files in this course show exactly this — not because any strength is a flaw, but because any strength, used without awareness of the moment, can become the wrong tool for it. Part of what this course asks is not "what is your strength" but "when might your strength need a partner?"

BLUF — How This Course Talks to You

BLUF — Bottom Line Up Front — is the standard USACE communication norm, and it is also how this course is written. Consequences come before explanations. Explanations lead with what matters most. You will see this pattern in how strong responses are written throughout the case studies — once you notice it here, you will notice it everywhere else in the course too.

Finding Your Way Around

A few things worth knowing before you start:

• The Text: S/M/L button adjusts text size across every page in this course — your preference is remembered.
• The theme toggle switches between light and dark mode.
• Progress is tracked per-browser, on your device — there is no login and no central record.
• The PDBP Lifecycle Map and PM's Toolbelt are reference pages you can return to any time — every PROC/REF citation and real-world tool mentioned in a case file links back to one of these.
