Situation Setup
SAYRead slowly: "Maria has just been assigned to a project already underway. The charter lists the previous PM's name. A cost review is in 10 days. She has not yet met most of her PDT." Pause. "Before we look at what PROC2020 requires — what's your first instinct? What do you do first?" Take two or three responses.
WATCH FOR"Call the DPM" — note it. "Find the charter" — reinforce it. "Meet the PDT" — acknowledge it. All three are defensible.
Concept Part A — What PROC2020 Requires
SAY"Named individuals — not 'TBD.' A name. Because when the project is in trouble at 4pm Friday, you need to know whose phone to call." "Decision authority thresholds — specific dollar amounts or scope changes beyond which someone needs higher approval." "Functional chief confirmation — the formal commitment that this person is available for the duration." "RACI — if you ask three people who owns a decision and get three different answers, you have a charter problem."
Concept Part B — MP Specific
MP Delivers"In Military Programs, charters sometimes outlive three PM transitions without being updated." Personalize from your own experience. The slide gives the framework; you give the credibility. This is the Military Programs-specific gap — a charter that reflects who the PM was two years ago, not who is in the seat today.
SAY — Both Instructors"The Two Accountables problem — raise your hand if you've been in a meeting where two people both thought they owned a decision. That's a charter problem. Not a personality problem. A documentation problem." "The simplest advice in this course: read the charter. If it hasn't been updated since the previous PM — that is your first action item."
IFSomeone says "we don't really use charters": "PROC2020 requires it. The question is whether you're getting the benefit of it."
The Skill — Read a Charter
SAY"Handouts face-down. Flip them over. Sample charter, one page. Five minutes. Work through the five steps. I want to hear what you'd update first." Let them work. At 3 min: "One more minute." At 5 min: "Table 1 — what's the first thing you'd fix?"

Lesson 2 · 30 min · CW + MP Support
Scope and Sponsor Alignment MP Delivers DD1391 Line
Initiation · PROC2010
▲
Concept — MP Specific
MP Delivers"The DD1391 describes what Congress approved — not always what the user actually needs." This is the Military Programs-specific scope gap: a DD1391 is written at a summary level, often years before design begins. The PM and the A-E have to translate that summary into what actually gets designed and built. If that translation isn't documented, the user's current expectation and the DD1391 description can diverge significantly.
SAY — Scope Statement"Scope statement is not a project description. The description says what you're building. The scope statement defines what IS and IS NOT included." "Three questions that change conversations: 'What's included?' 'What's explicitly excluded?' 'What happens if we find out later that X was not included?'"
WATCH FORMilitary Programs PMs who have dealt with a user who said "but that's what the DD1391 said" when the design came back different from their expectation. That's the scenario. Let them connect to it before you open CF02.

If scope expands during design, the user may expect the original date to hold. That's the tension. Lesson 3 · 30 min · CW + MP Support
The WBS and Scope in Practice MP Delivers DD1391 Line
Initiation · PROC2020
▲
MP Delivers — WBS Concept"The DD1391 describes what Congress approved at a summary level. The WBS is where the PM and A-E translate that into what actually gets designed and built." This is the Military Programs-specific gap: the DD1391 to WBS translation is where scope assumptions get surfaced — or don't. If the WBS skips this translation, the first the user hears about scope assumptions is during design review.
SAY"WBS stands for Work Breakdown Structure. It is not a schedule. It is not a task list. It is a hierarchical decomposition of scope into deliverable components." "Without the WBS, scope lives in documents. With it, scope lives in the work breakdown — where it can be scheduled, resourced, and tracked."

Planning · PROC2040
▲
SAY"Three things you need to know before you can have an informed conversation about money: where the number came from, what's in it, and what your options are when it doesn't fit the ceiling."
MP Delivers — SIOH"SIOH — Supervision, Inspection, and Overhead. In Military Programs typically 6.5% of construction cost. It comes from the same appropriation as the construction cost. Lesson 7 · 30 min · MP Leads
Measuring Performance — EVM MP Leads
Execution · PROC3000
▲
SAY — Lesson Open"EVM — Earned Value Management. We're not making you EVM experts today. We're making sure you can read the numbers, explain what they mean, and have an informed conversation when Col. Ashford calls on Monday morning before you've reviewed the data."
SAY — Three Values"Three values. PV — Planned Value — what was supposed to be accomplished by today in dollars. EV — Earned Value — what was actually accomplished in dollars. AC — Actual Cost — what was actually spent." SV = EV minus PV. CV = EV minus AC. SPI = EV divided by PV. CPI = EV divided by AC.
MP Delivers — Whiteboard ExampleWalk the whiteboard example if not fully covered in Part A. Use a real Military Programs construction scenario if possible — a barracks renovation or a motor pool. The numbers don't have to be exact; they have to be believable. The goal is for participants to calculate SPI, CPI, SV, and CV themselves before the case file opens.
MP Delivers — Col. Ashford Line"The portfolio rollup goes to Col. Ashford Monday morning. She calls Monday afternoon. You haven't reviewed the data. CF07 is exactly this moment." This is experiential and it's real. Military Programs participants have been in this call or know someone who has. Deliver it from that place.
WATCH FORParticipants who have been on the receiving end of a Monday portfolio review call. If one wants to share a 60-second story, let them. It primes the room for CF07 better than any setup you can write.

Lesson 9 · 30 min · SF Leads · MP Support
Running PDT Meetings MP Delivers Culture Line
Execution
▲
ROLE NOTESF leads this lesson. MP delivers the military discipline line in Concept Part B. This contrast between military and civilian PDT meeting cultures is real and recognized.
MP Delivers — Meeting Culture Line"In the military, meeting discipline comes built in. In a civilian PDT, you build it yourself — or you don't, and the meeting runs long." This is the Military Programs-specific tension: PMs who came from a military background may expect meeting discipline to be self-enforcing. It isn't. The PM builds it, explicitly, through agenda structure and time management. Personalize from your experience with civilian PDT members who don't share that expectation.
SAY — Three Meeting Types"Decision items go first. Time-sensitive items go second. Informational items go last or get cut. The key: informational items are never worth running over time for."
SAY — Time-Check Move"'We have eight minutes and two items — which must be decided today?' This puts the prioritization back on the team. They own the answer. You just named the constraint."
Lesson 10 · 30 min · MP Leads
Change Management MP Leads · Delivers KO Line
M&C · PROC3010
▲
SAY — Lesson Open"PROC3010. The approval chain. And the one thing that more PMs get wrong than almost anything else: thinking that verbal approval is approval."
SAY — What Constitutes a Change"PROC3010: a change is any modification to the approved scope, schedule, or cost baseline that has not been formally authorized. Four words that matter: 'has not been formally authorized.' Verbal doesn't count. Email doesn't count. The form counts."
MP Delivers — Concept Part B"In Military Programs, the KO processes the contract modification. The PM identifies the need and documents it — cannot independently authorize a scope change." This is the Military Programs-specific line: the PM and the KO are two different people with two different authorities. The PM's job is to identify, document, and bring the change to the KO with enough analysis that the KO can act. The PM does not cross into contract modification authority.
WATCH FORMilitary Programs PMs who have been in the situation where a contractor "just did" a change and the PM had to decide whether to document it or pretend it didn't happen. That's the gray zone CF10 explores. If someone nods or starts to share, let them go 60 seconds.
IFSomeone asks "what if the KO is slow?" — "The PM's job is to get the documentation to the KO. The KO's job is the authorization. If the KO is the bottleneck, that's a communication problem you solve before it's an emergency, not during."
Case File 10 — After Lesson 10
The Wrong Shade of Gray
MP DebriefIn Military Programs, "is this a change?" has a second question: "Does this require a contract modification?" The PM identifies and documents. The KO determines what modification is needed. If the PM short-circuits that process — even with good intent — the contract is now out of scope with no authorization. That's a problem the PM owns.

Lesson 11 · 30 min · CW Leads · MP Support
Risk as Opportunity — REF8007G MP Delivers Favorable Bid Line
M&C · REF8007G
▲
ROLE NOTECW leads this lesson. MP delivers the favorable bid line in Concept Part B. This is a recognized Military Programs situation.
SAY — Opportunity Register"Most Risk Registers have threats. REF8007G requires both. The tool is the same — the same fields, the same process. What changes is the orientation and the response strategies." Response strategies for opportunities: exploit, enhance, share, accept.
SAY — Second-Order Check"Second-order check: 'What else does this affect?' One question. Thirty seconds. Sometimes saves three weeks." This is the key behavioral habit — before acting on an opportunity, run the check.
MP Delivers — Favorable Bid Line"In Military Programs, a favorable bid under estimate is an opportunity. Scope-expanding to use the savings is a change process — not a free decision." This is the temptation: a bid comes in $400K under estimate, and someone suggests adding features the user has wanted but that weren't in the DD1391. That is a scope change. It goes through the change process. The savings don't create authority to expand scope. Personalize from your experience.
WATCH FORMilitary Programs participants who have experienced a favorable bid — either where scope was expanded correctly through the change process, or where it wasn't. Both are instructive.

Lesson 13 · 30 min · CW + MP Co-Lead
Closing the Project — PROC4000 and the AAR MP Co-Leads · DD1354
Closeout · PROC4000
▲
SAY — Lesson Open"The last lesson. PROC4000 and the AAR. Two things that often get rushed or skipped — and both of them are what the next team inherits."
SAY — Situation SetupRead slowly: "This is the last hour they will all be in the same room." Pause. That landing is intentional.
MP Delivers — PROC4000 ChecklistWalk the Military Programs-specific closeout items: construction completion certification, as-built drawings, O&M manuals, DD1354 (Transfer and Acceptance of Military Real Property — this is the formal transfer document from USACE to the installation), financial closeout, contractor performance evaluation (CPARS entry). Each item has a downstream user. The DD1354 is especially important — it formally transfers responsibility. If it's not done correctly, the installation doesn't formally own the facility.
SAY — AAR Principles"Leader speaks last — so they don't anchor the discussion before it starts. Blame is not the point — transferable learning is. Specifics over generalities — 'we should communicate better' is not a lesson. 'We need a standing agenda item for scope questions in every PDT meeting' is a lesson."
MP Live AAR ParticipationSF facilitates the live AAR on Day 1 of the course. CW and MP participate honestly — not as instructors, as participants who lived Day 1. Rules: leader speaks last, no blame, specifics only, every lesson needs an owner and a timeframe. After the debrief, MP adds: what does closing well look like in Military Programs? What's the one thing most often rushed in a MILCON closeout? Answer honestly.
