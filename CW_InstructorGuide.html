Situation Setup
SAYRead slowly: "Maria has just been assigned to a project already underway. The charter lists the previous PM's name. A cost review is in 10 days. She has not yet met most of her PDT." Pause after the last sentence. "Before we look at what PROC2020 requires — what's your first instinct? What do you do first?"
WATCH FOR"Call the DPM" — note it. "Find the charter" — reinforce it. "Meet the PDT" — acknowledge it. All three are defensible. The case file will test them.
Concept Part A — What PROC2020 Requires
SAYWork each bullet with a brief example. "Named individuals — not 'the structural engineer,' not 'TBD.' A name. Because when the project is in trouble at 4pm Friday, you need to know whose phone to call." "Decision authority thresholds — the specific dollar amount beyond which someone needs higher approval. Without it, every decision is either over-cleared or under-cleared." "Functional chief confirmation — the formal commitment that this person is available for the duration." "RACI — if you ask three people who owns a decision and get three different answers, you have a RACI problem."
WATCH FORParticipants writing — they're mapping this to their own project. Participants who look uncomfortable — they don't have these documented.
Concept Part B — CW Specific
CW Delivers"In Civil Works, the sponsor is often on the PDT with formal cost-share obligations in the PPA." Personalize from your own experience — the slide gives the framework; you give the credibility.
SAY — Both Instructors"The Two Accountables problem — raise your hand if you've been in a meeting where two people both thought they owned a decision. That's a charter problem. Not a personality problem. A documentation problem." Then: "The simplest advice in this course: read the charter. If there isn't one, or if it hasn't been updated since the previous PM — that is your first action item. Not second. First."
IFSomeone says "we don't really use charters in our district": "Then this lesson is especially for you. PROC2020 requires it. The question is whether you're getting the benefit of it."
The Skill — Read a Charter
SAY"Handouts face-down until now. Flip them over. Sample charter, one page. Five minutes. Work through the five steps on screen. I want to hear what you'd update first." Go quiet. Let them work. At 3 minutes: "One more minute." At 5 minutes: "Table 1 — what's the first thing you'd fix?"
WATCH FORSomeone who identifies a missing decision authority threshold — that's the key insight. If someone says the charter looks fine: "Walk me through what you saw. What's in the PM field? What's the decision threshold?" They'll find the gap.

Concept — CW Specific

SAY — Scope Statement"Scope statement is not a project description. The description says what you're building. The scope statement defines what IS and IS NOT included. Two different documents with two different jobs."

Lesson 3 · 30 min · CW + MP Support
The WBS and Scope in Practice
Initiation · PROC2020
▲
SAY"WBS stands for Work Breakdown Structure. It is not a schedule. It is not a task list. It is a hierarchical decomposition of scope into deliverable components." "Without the WBS, scope lives in documents. With it, scope lives in the work breakdown — where it can be scheduled, resourced, and tracked."
WATCH FORParticipants who say their project "doesn't have a WBS" — that means their schedule is built on assumptions they can't see. Don't correct harshly; acknowledge that the WBS is often skipped under pressure. The case file shows what that costs.

Lesson 5 · 30 min · CW + MP Co-Lead
The Cost Estimate CW Leads Budget Context
Planning · PROC2040
▲
SAY"Three things you need to know before you can have an informed conversation about money on your project: where the number came from, what's in it, and what your options are when it doesn't fit the ceiling."
SAY — Funding Ceiling vs. Project Cost"Funding ceiling vs. project cost. These are not the same number. The ceiling is what was programmed. The project cost is what it actually takes to deliver the scope. When those diverge — you have decisions to make."
CW Delivers — Budget Cycle ContextDeliver the Civil Works budget cycle context: the multi-year congressional appropriation cycle, how cost increases require supplemental budget requests, the CWIS and budget year submission implications, and what it means when a project "breaks" its programmed cost. This is genuinely different from MP — CW participants need to hear their own program's reality. Personalize from your experience.
WATCH FORCW participants who have faced this exact situation — "$2.3M over ceiling, make it work." They'll nod or share. Let one go briefly. "That's exactly what CF05 is about."

Lesson 8 · 30 min · CW Leads
Risk Management — The Risk Register CW Leads
Execution · REF8007G
▲
SAY — Lesson Open"REF8007G is the governing reference. We're going to look at every required field in the Risk Register and practice the fastest way to capture a risk before it walks out of the room."
SAY — Three Categories"Three categories first — Risk, Assumption, Issue. Getting these right matters because they require different responses. A risk has probability and impact. An assumption is something you're treating as true without verification. An issue is a risk that's already happened."
SAY — The Trigger Field"The trigger is the most neglected field in most Risk Registers. Without a trigger, a risk sits on the Register being 'monitored' — which means nothing is happening. A trigger is a specific, observable event that tells you the risk is materializing. It converts passive monitoring into active watching."
CW Delivers — Specialty Item Risk"Vendor delivery risk on specialty items is one of the most common unlogged risks in Civil Works projects. It lives in someone's head until it doesn't — until the specialty mechanical unit that has a 14-week lead time is ordered in week 10 of a 16-week execution window." This is specific and it's recognized immediately by Civil Works PMs.
WATCH FORParticipants who start writing risk entries for their current project during the skill exercise. That's the goal. Someone who re-frames an assumption as a risk — "I assumed the contractor would have the form-work crew — that's actually a risk" — name that publicly.

Lesson 10 · 30 min · MP Leads · CW Delivers Key Line
Change Management CW — Cost-Share Changes
M&C · PROC3010
▲
ROLE NOTEMP leads this lesson. CW delivers the Civil Works cost-share change line in Concept Part B. This is a genuinely different process from MP — be specific.
CW Delivers — Concept Part B"In Civil Works, changes affecting the sponsor's cost-share obligations require additional process beyond the district. Know before you act." Explain: a scope change that bumps the total project cost triggers a cost-share recalculation. The sponsor has to agree. If the PPA doesn't cover it, you need an amendment. This is not just a paperwork step — it can delay execution by weeks. MP PMs don't deal with this. Civil Works PMs do, every time.
WATCH FORCivil Works participants nodding — they've been here. If someone shares a story about an unauthorized change that created a sponsor problem, let them go for 60 seconds then: "That's exactly the gap CF10 explores."

Lesson 11 · 30 min · CW Leads
Risk as Opportunity — REF8007G CW Leads
M&C · REF8007G
▲
SAY — Lesson Open"The other half of REF8007G. Most teams log threats. Few log opportunities. Same tool, different orientation. Thirty minutes on opportunity management and the one check that prevents an opportunity from becoming its own problem."
SAY — Opportunity Register"Most Risk Registers have threats. REF8007G requires both. The tool is the same — the same fields, the same process. What changes is the orientation and the response strategies." Response strategies for opportunities: exploit, enhance, share, accept.
SAY — Second-Order Check"Second-order check: 'What else does this affect?' One question. Thirty seconds. Sometimes saves three weeks." This is the key behavioral habit — before acting on an opportunity, run the check.
CW Delivers — Early Sponsor Sign-Off"In Civil Works, early sponsor sign-off is an opportunity that requires immediate action — the sponsor's availability windows are narrow." If the sponsor can get to the site for a final acceptance inspection two weeks early, that's real schedule recovery — but it requires immediate coordination, not a standing agenda item. This is a recognized situation. Personalize it.
WATCH FORParticipants who have experienced a "windfall opportunity" — a bid that came in well under estimate, early contractor availability — and didn't know how to run it through the change process. "That's a gift from the project, but it's still a change."

Lesson 13 · 30 min · CW + MP Co-Lead
Closing the Project — PROC4000 and the AAR CW Co-Leads
Closeout · PROC4000
▲
SAY — Lesson Open"The last lesson. PROC4000 and the AAR. Two things that often get rushed or skipped — and both of them are what the next team inherits."
SAY — Situation SetupRead slowly: "This is the last hour they will all be in the same room." Pause. That landing is intentional.
CW Delivers — PROC4000 ChecklistWalk the Civil Works-specific closeout items: sponsor handoff documentation, cost-share reconciliation (final cost vs. programmed cost, any supplemental billing), real estate instrument releases if applicable, and sponsor acceptance of completed work. Each one has a counterpart in the sponsor relationship. The handoff to the sponsor is a formal event — not just a walkthrough.
SAY — AAR Principles"Leader speaks last — so they don't anchor the discussion before it starts. Blame is not the point — the point is transferable learning. Specifics over generalities — 'we should communicate better' is not a lesson. 'We need a standing agenda item for scope questions in every PDT meeting' is a lesson."
CW Live AAR ParticipationSF facilitates the live AAR on Day 1 of the course. CW and MP participate honestly — not as instructors, as participants who lived Day 1. Rules: leader speaks last, no blame, specifics only, every lesson needs an owner and a timeframe. After the debrief on the AAR process, CW adds: what does closing well look like in Civil Works? What's the one thing most often rushed in a Civil Works project closeout? Answer honestly.
